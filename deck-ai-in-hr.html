What we covered

Three ways of working
you can try tomorrow

Every topic followed the same pattern: a simple example of the opportunity, then a short workflow with a prompt you can copy and run. The method behind all three is the same: connect the right information, analyze it with AI, and act on a clear output.

Connect, Analyze, Act

The core method: combine data, documents and conversations, use AI to find patterns, gaps and alternatives, and turn the result into a dashboard, recommendation or practice workflow. The value is in the workflow, not the prompt.

Business results + people insights

Business challenges rarely have one explanation. Bringing manager conversations, business data and people data into one analysis surfaces trends, possible explanations and the information that is still missing.

From a conversation to a dashboard

A live demo with synthetic data: a manager's conversation plus CRM and HR data, analyzed by AI and turned into a management dashboard, with facts kept separate from hypotheses.

Analyze and practice conversations

Analyze, identify gaps, improve, practice. AI helps examine what was said and what was assumed, then becomes a role-play partner for the next conversation, without claiming to know anyone's intentions.

A personal AI learning companion

Same organizational knowledge, different learning experiences. Organizational knowledge, personal preferences and ongoing practice turn one AI assistant into support adapted to each person's role, level, language and pace.

Measure what improves

Problem, workflow, validate, measure. Start small, connect the right information, and measure better understanding, clearer communication and real learning progress, not just how often AI is used.

Quick reference

Before you start
and how to measure value

Three short checklists worth returning to. Unlike the slides, the text below can be selected and copied.

Use AI responsibly

Four rules that apply to every workflow from the session.

  1. Work in the AI tools your organization has approved, and follow its AI policy.
  2. Use synthetic or anonymized data. Don't enter personal employee information unless it's explicitly approved.
  3. Check facts, numbers and conclusions before you share or act on them.
  4. AI supports the thinking. Decisions about people stay with people.
A shared trend is a reason to investigate, not proof of cause.

From experiment to organizational value

Start small. Connect the right information. Measure what improves.

  1. Problem: identify what needs to improve.
  2. Workflow: combine AI with the right inputs.
  3. Validate: review the output and its limitations.
  4. Measure: evaluate the actual impact.
Measure what improves, not just how often AI is used.

What to measure

Examples from the three workflows.

  1. Business insights: better understanding, faster analysis.
  2. Conversation coaching: clearer communication, better preparation.
  3. Personalized learning: relevant learning, measurable progress.
Define success before you start, and compare against a meaningful baseline.

More prompts: business insights

09 Prepare for a business leader conversation Insights 
Act as an experienced HR business partner. I'm meeting [ROLE] about [TOPIC]. Here is what I know so far: [SUMMARY OR DATA].

Help me prepare: list what we know, what we're assuming, and what is missing. Then suggest five questions I should ask to understand the business problem before proposing any solution.
10 Find themes in open-ended feedback Insights 
Analyze the anonymized feedback below. Group the comments into 5–7 themes, show how many comments support each theme, and quote two short representative examples per theme. Flag comments that don't fit any theme. Don't try to infer who wrote any comment.

[PASTE ANONYMIZED COMMENTS]
11 Review a report before you share it Insights 
Review the attached report as a critical colleague. Which conclusions are well supported by the data? Which claims are weak or unsupported? What questions would a skeptical executive ask? Suggest what to add or remove before I share it.
12 Turn an analysis into a decision brief Insights 
Turn the analysis above into a one-page brief for [AUDIENCE]: the problem in two sentences, key findings, options with pros and cons, a recommended next step, and open questions. Keep it under 300 words and label assumptions clearly.

More prompts: conversations

13 Prepare a feedback conversation Conversations 
Help me prepare for a feedback conversation with [ROLE]. The situation: [FACTS ONLY].

Separate observable facts from my interpretations. Suggest a clear and respectful opening, three open questions to understand their perspective, and a way to agree on next steps. Don't assume anything about the person's intentions or personality.
14 Make a message clear for a global team Conversations 
Rewrite the message below for colleagues in different countries who work in English as a second language. Use short sentences and plain words, put the main point first, and make the request, owner and deadline explicit. Then list anything that could still be misunderstood.

[PASTE YOUR MESSAGE]
15 Debrief a meeting Conversations 
From the meeting notes below, list the decisions made, action items with owners and dates, open questions, and points where participants may have understood things differently. If an owner or date is missing, mark it as TBD. Don't guess.

[PASTE MEETING NOTES]
16 Rehearse a challenging stakeholder Conversations 
Play a [ROLE, e.g. skeptical department head] who disagrees with my proposal to [PROPOSAL]. Push back realistically, one message at a time. After five exchanges, step out of the role and tell me which arguments worked, which didn't, and what I should prepare better.

More prompts: learning and support

17 Explain it at my level Learning 
Using only the attached [POLICY OR PROCEDURE], explain [TOPIC] to someone who is [NEW TO THE ROLE / EXPERIENCED]. Start with a three-sentence summary, then give a short example from daily work. If the document doesn't cover something, say so. Don't fill the gap with assumptions.
18 Build a 30-60-90 day onboarding plan Learning 
Create a 30-60-90 day onboarding plan for a new [ROLE] in [TEAM]. For each period, include goals, key people to meet, knowledge to acquire, and a simple way to check progress. If important information is missing, ask me up to three questions first.
19 Turn a document into practice Learning 
Turn the attached document into a short learning exercise: five questions based on realistic work situations, with the correct answer and a one-line explanation for each. Base everything only on the document.
20 Create a personal learning plan Learning 
I want to improve [SKILL]. I have [TIME PER WEEK] and prefer [FORMAT, e.g. short exercises, reading, practice conversations]. Ask me three questions about my current level, then create a four-week plan with one small practice task per week and a simple way to measure progress.

More prompts: working well with AI

21 Ask me first Any task 
Before you answer, ask me up to five questions that would help you understand my goal, audience, context and quality standard. Wait for my answers before you start.
22 Challenge your own answer Any task 
Review your previous answer critically. What might be wrong, missing or based on assumptions? What would an expert in [FIELD] disagree with? Then give me an improved version and list what I should still verify myself.
23 Save it as a reusable template Any task 
Turn the process we just went through into a reusable prompt template I can use again. Use [PLACEHOLDERS] for the parts that change each time, and add a short checklist for reviewing the output.
