Security is not a single product feature — it is the combination of the environment you work in, the information you share and the habits you keep. These are the threads that run through the whole workshop.

Quick reference

Keep this open
during your first week

The two checklists worth returning to. Unlike the slides, the text below can be selected and copied — take it into your own notes or team documentation.

The 30-second check

Before you paste, attach or share anything.

  1. Am I using the approved work account and the protected organizational experience?
  2. Is this information permitted — and does Copilot really need all of it?
  3. Can I anonymize, summarize or remove irrelevant details first?
  4. Who will verify the answer and approve the final use or sharing?
Not every piece of information that can be uploaded should be uploaded.

Eight habits for safer everyday AI use

These apply to every AI tool, not only Copilot.

  1. Use the approved organizational account and verify the protection indicator.
  2. Classify the information before entering, pasting or uploading it.
  3. Remove names, identifiers, client details and irrelevant sections whenever possible.
  4. Provide only the minimum information required to complete the task.
  5. Do not enter passwords, credentials, secrets or restricted personal information.
  6. Ask for sources, page references and explicit uncertainty when accuracy matters.
  7. Review every output before using it in a decision, document or external communication.
  8. Check sharing permissions before sending a chat link, Page, workbook or generated file.
If you are unsure whether information is allowed, stop and ask the responsible manager, privacy lead or IT team.

Find Copilot in the apps you already use

Look for the Copilot icon in the ribbon, the chat pane or the lower-right corner. In any app, ask: "What can Copilot help me do in this application?"

  1. Excel — explore data, create formulas, identify trends, build charts and turn a table into a visual business view.
  2. PowerPoint — create a draft presentation, improve structure, rewrite slides, add visuals and adapt content to an audience.
  3. Word — draft, rewrite, summarize and transform source material into structured documents.
  4. Outlook, Teams and more — summarize permitted content, prepare communication and identify actions without changing environments.
The best place to use Copilot is often inside the application where the source information and final output already belong.

16 prompts

Set Copilot up for you

01 Introduce your role to Copilot Saved memory

Copy, complete and submit the following prompt using only non-sensitive information.

From now on, remember for future conversations that my role is [ROLE] at [ORGANIZATION / ORGANIZATION TYPE].

My core responsibilities are [RESPONSIBILITIES]. Tasks I perform frequently include [RECURRING TASKS].

I usually work with [STAKEHOLDERS / AUDIENCES]. The outcomes I am responsible for include [OUTCOMES].

Before saving anything, list the details you intend to remember and ask me to confirm. Do not store confidential, personal, client, legal, HR, security or commercially sensitive information.

After Copilot confirms, ask: "What do you remember about me, and what important non-sensitive context is still missing?"

Keep working style and response rules for Custom Instructions — not as approximate memories.

02 Teach Copilot about your organization Web research

Replace the placeholders. Use only publicly available information, and ask Copilot to verify before it proposes anything for memory.

Research [ORGANIZATION] using the public web. Prioritize its official website and recent trustworthy sources.

Summarize its mission, main activities, audiences, products or services, public priorities and communication style.

Cite links and publication dates. Separate verified facts, reasonable inferences and open questions. Flag conflicting or outdated information.

Ask me to correct the summary. Only after I confirm it, propose up to five non-sensitive facts that may be useful to remember. Do not save anything until I explicitly approve.

Finish by asking: "Which of these facts is stable enough for memory, and which should remain only in this conversation?"

Public does not automatically mean accurate, current or appropriate for permanent memory.

03 Recommended Custom Instructions for everyday work Copy-ready

Custom Instructions answer "How should you work with me?" — while memory answers "What stable, non-sensitive facts should you know about me?" Paste this into Copilot settings as your starting point.

01 Start with the conclusion, recommendation or requested result. Then provide the reasoning and practical next steps.

02 When essential information is missing, ask focused clarification questions instead of inventing facts.

03 Clearly separate verified facts, assumptions, interpretations and recommendations.

04 For external or time-sensitive claims, cite reliable sources and state when the information was checked.

05 Challenge weak assumptions, identify risks and offer alternatives instead of agreeing automatically.

06 Use clear, professional and direct English. Adapt terminology and depth to the stated audience.

07 Flag anything that requires human judgment, verification, privacy review or managerial approval.

08 Prefer copy-ready outputs: a concise message, structured table, checklist, decision brief or action plan.

Review these instructions and adapt them to your role, organization and approved AI policy.

04 Ask Copilot to design your Custom Instructions Personalized

Use what Copilot already knows — but require a short discovery step before it writes the final instructions.

Based on our conversations and saved memories, ask me three focused questions about how I want you to communicate, challenge my thinking and structure outputs.

Then recommend custom instructions under five headings: response style, structure, reliability, critical thinking and privacy boundaries.

Provide one concise version that I can paste directly into Copilot settings. Keep it specific, practical and free of generic slogans.

Do not include sensitive organizational facts. Highlight any instruction that could create a risk or reduce flexibility, and ask for my approval before finalizing.

After saving, test the instructions with one familiar task and compare the result with an earlier answer.

Custom Instructions are a default — not a substitute for the context and requirements of the current task.

Work a real task

05 Develop a real project with Think Deeper Think Deeper

Select Think Deeper / GPT Thinking, then complete the placeholders.

I need to advance [PROJECT / TASK]. The current situation is [CONTEXT]. The desired outcome is [GOAL].

Stakeholders are [PEOPLE]. The target date is [DATE]. Constraints, dependencies and available resources include [DETAILS].

First ask up to five focused questions. Do not assume facts I have not provided. Clearly label assumptions and decisions that require approval.

Provide an executive summary, workstreams, milestones, owners, dependencies, risks, success measures, open questions and the next three actions.

After the first draft, ask: "Critique this plan as a skeptical sponsor. What is unrealistic, missing or insufficiently evidenced?"

Use permitted project information only. Replace sensitive names, figures and identifiers with safe placeholders.

06 Turn a vague work request into a clear plan Request → action

Paste a permitted request, message or assignment, then use this prompt.

Explain in one paragraph what is being requested, the desired outcome, the likely audience and any explicit constraints.

Identify ambiguities, missing information, hidden assumptions, dependencies and decisions that require clarification.

Draft up to five concise questions I should ask before starting. Prioritize questions that could materially change the work.

After I answer, create a practical action plan and a short reply confirming the scope, responsibilities, timeline and next action.

Follow-up: "What could I misunderstand if I begin the task without answering these questions?"

Copilot should help clarify the task before it helps complete the task.

07 One task. One conversation. Several useful outputs. Same chat · 8–10 min

Four steps in a single conversation. Send them one after another without starting a new chat.

01 START WITH THE REAL TASK
I need to complete [TASK]. The context is [CONTEXT], the audience is [AUDIENCE], and a good result should achieve [OUTCOME]. Ask me up to four questions, then create a first draft.

02 CHALLENGE AND IMPROVE
Review the draft critically. What is missing, unclear, unsupported or unlikely to work? Ask for any essential information and then revise it.

03 CREATE A COMMUNICATION OUTPUT
Using the same context, create a concise email for [MANAGER / COLLEAGUES / PARTICIPANTS]. Start with the key message and end with a clear requested action.

04 CREATE A MANAGEMENT OUTPUT
Convert the final plan into an Excel-ready action table with task, owner, due date, dependency, status and success measure. Do not invent missing owners or dates.

Do not restart the chat between steps. Each follow-up should reuse the context, decisions and corrections already collected.

08 Build your personal Copilot opportunity map Role-based discovery

Use the context already collected in memory and ask Copilot to prioritize value, feasibility and risk.

Based on what you know about my role and responsibilities, ask me up to five questions that will help identify valuable Copilot use cases.

Then list, for each opportunity: task, current friction, how Copilot can help, required input, expected output, frequency, estimated time saving and main risk.

Score each opportunity from 1-5 for value, ease of implementation and risk. Explain the scoring and select the top three.

For the two best opportunities, provide a step-by-step pilot I can run this week, including a safe input, prompt, quality check and success measure.

Do not recommend automation before the manual Copilot-assisted process has been tested and reviewed.

09 10-minute mini-sprint Same chat · 10–12 min

One task, one conversation, four improvements. Keep the same chat throughout.

01 CHOOSE
Based on what you know about my role, suggest three low-risk recurring tasks where Copilot could save time this week. Recommend one and explain why.

02 CLARIFY
Before solving it, ask me three focused questions about the goal, audience, available information and quality standard.

03 CREATE
Produce the first usable output. Include a short checklist I can use to verify accuracy, completeness and privacy.

04 IMPROVE AND TRANSFER
Critique the output, revise it, and convert the final result into either a Page-ready document or an Excel-ready table.

Success = a real output, a clear quality check and one next step you can repeat after the workshop.

Inside the Microsoft 365 apps

10 Build a business dashboard in Excel Excel

Open a permitted workbook, select the Copilot icon in the lower-right corner or ribbon, and use this prompt.

Review the data structure, column meanings and data quality. Identify missing values, duplicates, inconsistent dates or fields that may prevent reliable analysis.

Create a business dashboard on a new worksheet for [AUDIENCE]. The purpose is to understand [BUSINESS QUESTION].

Include KPI cards for [KPIs], a trend chart over time, a comparison by [CATEGORY], top and bottom performers, and useful filters or slicers.

Do not invent missing data. Explain each metric and formula, compare the dashboard totals with the source table, and flag anything that requires human review.

Follow-up: "What are the three most important insights in this dashboard, and which business question should we investigate next?"

A beautiful dashboard is not useful unless its metrics, formulas and source data have been verified.

11 Create a first-draft presentation in PowerPoint PowerPoint

Start with a blank presentation or the approved organizational template, open Copilot, and complete this prompt.

Create a [NUMBER]-slide presentation about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE]. The desired outcome is [DECISION / UNDERSTANDING / ACTION].

Use [PERMITTED FILE / OUTLINE / NOTES] as the source. Do not add unsupported facts, and clearly mark information that requires verification.

Build a clear flow: context, problem, key insight, recommendation, implementation steps and final call to action.

Use one main idea per slide, concise text, meaningful headings, suggested visuals and speaker notes. Follow the existing template and brand style.

Follow-up: "Review the presentation as a skeptical audience member. Which slides are unclear, repetitive or insufficiently supported?"

Copilot creates a draft. The presenter remains responsible for the message, evidence, design and final delivery.

Make it reusable

12 Crack & Save: turn a good conversation into a reusable prompt Copy-ready

Use this only after the task has been completed and the result is genuinely good.

Review our full conversation and identify the context, questions, instructions, corrections, decisions and quality checks that led to the final result.

Create one reusable prompt that includes role, required inputs, task steps, constraints, output structure and questions to ask when information is missing.

Remove sensitive or one-time details. Replace names, dates, figures and organization-specific facts with clear placeholders.

Provide a short version and a detailed version, a suggested Prompt Gallery name, usage instructions and a checklist for testing the prompt next time.

Before saving, test the new prompt in a fresh chat and compare the result with the successful original conversation.

Do not save a prompt because it sounds good — save it because it reproduced a good result.

13 Design one Copilot Agent you can actually build One agent

Use what Copilot already knows about you. Ask for exactly one lightweight agent that fits the Basic experience.

Based on what you know about my role, responsibilities, recurring tasks and preferences, recommend exactly one Copilot Agent I can build in Microsoft 365 Copilot Agent Builder. If essential information is missing, ask me up to three focused questions first.

Choose a frequent, low-risk and repeatable process. Do not recommend advanced automation, unavailable organizational connectors or actions that require Copilot Studio.

Provide copy-ready text under these headings: agent name, short description, purpose, intended user, instructions, required user inputs, workflow steps, output format, boundaries and human-review rules.

Add permitted knowledge sources, three conversation starters, one realistic test task, success criteria and any assumption or feature that may require admin approval, Copilot Credits, pay-as-you-go or a different license.

One agent only. Make it copy-ready, then test it on a real, permitted task before sharing.

Optional labs

14 Analyze a document without overclaiming File + evidence

Attach a permitted file and replace the placeholders.

Analyze the attached file for [PURPOSE] and for an audience of [AUDIENCE].

First state what can be concluded, what cannot be concluded and which information is missing. Do not add external facts unless I explicitly request them.

Extract the main points, decisions, commitments, dates, owners, risks, contradictions and open questions.

For each important claim, cite the page, section or heading. Finish with an action table and a list of items that require human verification.

Follow-up: "Which three claims in your answer are most important to verify in the original document, and why?"

A confident answer without a traceable source is not a verified answer.

15 Turn permitted meeting notes into action Notes → Page / Excel

Paste only meeting notes that are approved for use.

Identify decisions, action items, owners, due dates, dependencies, open questions and unresolved disagreements.

Do not guess missing owners or dates. Mark them as TBD and list the clarification required.

Draft a concise follow-up email and an Excel-ready action table with columns: action, owner, due date, status, dependency and source note.

Compare both outputs with the original notes and flag anything that may have changed meaning.

Then move the narrative summary to Pages and the action tracker to the Excel Agent.

Never paste restricted meeting content into an environment that is not approved for that information.

16 Create a decision brief — not just a list of pros and cons Structured decision

Use Think Deeper when the choice has several criteria, trade-offs or stakeholders.

I need to choose between [OPTION A], [OPTION B] and [OPTION C] for [DECISION].

Evaluate cost, time, impact, feasibility, risk, stakeholder fit and [OTHER CRITERIA]. Ask questions before scoring.

Create a weighted comparison table. Show evidence, assumptions, uncertainty and the sensitivity of the result to different weights.

Recommend one option, explain the trade-off, present the strongest case against your recommendation and identify the required human approvals.

Follow-up: "What new evidence would be most likely to change the recommendation?"

Copilot can structure the decision. Accountability for the decision remains human.
